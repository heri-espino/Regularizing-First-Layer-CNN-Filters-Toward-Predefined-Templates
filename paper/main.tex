\author{}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Predefined first-layer filters provide an explicit structural prior, but preserving that structure need not make intervention-based measurements stable. We study small CNNs whose first convolution is initialized from a fixed bank of edge, corner, and ring templates and trained either with a constant $L_2$ penalty toward those templates or with the same penalty annealed to zero. Constant regularization strongly preserves filter-template similarity. Functionally, however, the annealed-minus-constant activation-patching comparison depends on how many first-layer channels are patched. In a prespecified prospective test on 20 fresh \texttt{two\_concepts}/TinyCNN blocks, the contrast comparing $k=4,8$ with $k=1,2$ is $B=0.33098$ (95\% CI $[0.27368,0.38827]$). A frozen metric-sensitivity analysis shows that the same qualitative contrast remains under centered-logit fidelity ($B=0.17888$) and an unnormalized probability-error reduction ($B=0.89441$), despite substantial treatment differences in the normalization scale of the original metric. A prospectively frozen architecture study on 100 fresh renderer blocks and 16 controlled CNN variants then finds large architecture heterogeneity under both primary metrics, with partial $\eta^2=0.438$ and $0.869$. The original TinyCNN--TwoLayer-form difference also replicates prospectively under both metrics. Pooling and the first downstream convolution are major modifiers, but their effects interact with backbone, normalization, task, and metric. Thus, high template similarity does not imply an architecture-invariant activation-patching signature. The claims are restricted to the tested synthetic tasks and small CNN family and do not establish a unique mechanism, human interpretability, or natural-image generalization.\footnote{ChatGPT (OpenAI) was used as an assistive tool during experimental planning, code development and debugging, manuscript organization, and language editing. All experimental protocols, analyses, interpretations, and manuscript text were reviewed by the authors, who take full responsibility for the work.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Predefined spatial filters can make the first convolutional layer structurally explicit. A different question is whether preserving that structure also stabilizes how internal activations appear to influence model outputs. These are not the same object: filter-template similarity is a weight-space property, whereas activation patching measures the response of a trained network to an intervention on internal activations.

We study this distinction in controlled synthetic image tasks with exact matched counterfactuals. The first convolution is initialized from a fixed bank of edge, corner, and ring filters. One training condition keeps an $L_2$ penalty toward the initial bank throughout training; another begins from the same bank but anneals the penalty to zero. Constant regularization retains substantially more template-like filters. We then patch selected first-layer activation maps from a matched counterfactual image into the base run and ask how the release-versus-retention comparison changes as the number of patched channels increases.

The central empirical result is that the answer is not stable across intervention size or downstream architecture. A prespecified prospective test confirms a strong channel-count contrast in TinyCNN on fresh renderer blocks. That contrast persists when the output comparison is changed from the original normalized probability metric to centered logits and to an unnormalized probability-space error reduction. A subsequent prospectively frozen study across 16 controlled CNN variants finds substantial architecture heterogeneity under both alternative metrics and prospectively reproduces the earlier TinyCNN-versus-TwoLayer-form difference.

This paper therefore makes a narrow measurement claim rather than a universal mechanistic one. We do not claim that sensitivity to activation-patching granularity is itself new, nor that predefined filters are automatically human-interpretable. Instead, we connect two questions that are often treated separately: whether a structured prior remains visible in weight space, and whether intervention-based functional comparisons remain stable as the intervention set and downstream architecture change. In our setting, they do not.

Our main findings are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Constant template regularization strongly preserves first-layer template similarity, but that structural retention does not determine the relative activation-patching effect.
\item The annealed-minus-constant patching comparison depends on the number of patched first-layer channels; a prespecified fresh-block TinyCNN test supports a positive small-versus-larger-channel contrast.
\item The TinyCNN contrast persists under centered-logit fidelity and an unnormalized probability reconstruction-error reduction, even though the original metric's model-specific normalization scale differs strongly between treatments.
\item Architecture is a major conditioning variable. Across 16 controlled CNN variants, the channel-count contrast varies substantially; pooling and the first downstream convolution are especially important, but their directions interact with the remaining architecture and the output metric.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Predefined and structured convolutional filters.}
Gabor filters and other predefined spatial filters have been used for initialization, constrained first layers, and learnable structured kernels \citep{ozbulak_2018_gabor-initialization,molaei_2020_gabor-filter-structure,wang_2024_learnable-gabor,linse_2024_predefined-filters,orozco-solis_2024_learnable-gabor-filters}. Fixed or predefined spatial filters can also support useful predictors when later layers learn how to combine them \citep{gaudio_2023_fixed-filters,gavrikov_2023_random-convolutions}. Our question is not whether these filters can be useful, but whether retaining their weight-space structure stabilizes a downstream intervention measurement.

\paragraph{Concept measurements and activation interventions.}
Network Dissection, TCAV, concept bottlenecks, concept embeddings, and related methods characterize concept correspondence or impose concept-aligned representations under different assumptions \citep{bau_2017_network-dissection,kim_2018_tcav,koh_2020_concept-bottlenecks,zarlenga_2022_concept-embeddings}. Weight-space template similarity should not be conflated with these activation-space notions.

Activation patching replaces internal activations and measures a downstream output change. Its conclusions can depend on corruption or replacement scheme, output metric, component choice, ablation method, and intervention granularity \citep{zhang_2024_activation-patching,heimersheim_2024_activation-patching,miller_2024_circuit-faithfulness}. We therefore do not claim granularity sensitivity as a new general phenomenon. Our narrower contribution is to hold the first-layer intervention space and matched counterfactual construction fixed while comparing two structured-prior training conditions, and then to test prospectively how the relative patching comparison changes across channel count and controlled downstream architectures. This is also consistent with the broader distinction between information being encoded in a representation and functionally influencing an output \citep{nicolson_2025_explaining-explainability,sharma_2026_encoding-without-influence}. Stronger claims about unique internal mechanisms require substantially more than the interventions used here \citep{meloux_2025_mechanistic-identifiability,sutter_2025_causal-abstraction-limits}.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Tasks, first-layer prior, and training conditions}\label{sec:setup}

We use two $32\times32$ grayscale procedural tasks with balanced labels, matched nuisance variables, and exact counterfactual pairs. Each renderer block contains 512 training, 256 validation, and 256 test images. Rendering varies angle, line width, scale, translation, Gaussian noise, and occasional occlusion. In \texttt{single\_shape}, the class is line, circle, triangle, or square. In \texttt{two\_concepts}, circle and triangle presence define two binary factors and four classes; counterfactual pairs change one factor while holding the other and the nuisance variables fixed.

\Cref{fig:overview} shows representative images from the frozen renderer, the complete 16-filter template bank, and the intervention point used throughout the paper.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig00_overview.pdf}
\caption{Overview of the controlled experimental setting. (a) Representative inputs produced by the same procedural renderer used in the experiments. (b) The fixed bank of eight edge, four corner, and four ring templates used to initialize the first convolution. (c) Activation patching replaces a selected set of $k$ first-layer activation maps from the base run with the corresponding maps from a matched counterfactual run, while leaving all downstream parameters unchanged. The displayed images are illustrative renderer outputs, not additional inferential observations.}
\label{fig:overview}
\end{figure}

Every model begins with the same bias-free $1\!\to\!16$ convolution with $9\times9$ kernels, same padding, and ReLU. The 16-channel template bank contains eight edges, four two-ray corners, and four rings. Templates are centered and unit norm; the bank has rank 10. For learned filter $W_i$ and template $T_j$, filter-template similarity is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:template-similarity}
A=\frac{1}{16}\sum_i\max_j\langle \widehat W_i,\widehat T_j\rangle.
\end{equation}
where the inner product is signed centered cosine similarity.

Template-based models initialize the first convolution from the bank and optimize
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:training-objective}
\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CE}}+
\lambda(e)\frac{\lVert W-W_{\mathrm{anchor}}\rVert_F^2}
{\lVert W_{\mathrm{anchor}}\rVert_F^2}.
\end{equation}
The central comparison is between \emph{constant template regularization}, $\lambda(e)=1$ for all 200 epochs, and \emph{annealed template regularization}, with $\lambda=1$ through epoch 10, linear decay to zero by epoch 80, and zero thereafter. Adam uses learning rate $0.003$ and batch size 128, with no learning-rate scheduler or early stopping.

\subsection{Activation patching and channel ranking}\label{sec:patching}

For each matched base/counterfactual pair, we run the model on the base image, the counterfactual image, and a patched base image. Patching occurs immediately after the first convolution and ReLU: selected first-layer activation maps from the base run are replaced by the corresponding maps from the counterfactual run. The downstream network is otherwise unchanged.

Channels are ranked on validation data by the absolute standardized difference in first-layer global-max activation,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:channel-ranking}
d_{g,j}=
\frac{\overline z_{g=1,j}-\overline z_{g=0,j}}
{s_j+10^{-6}}.
\end{equation}
where $z_j$ is the global maximum of first-layer activation map $j$ and $s_j$ is its validation-set standard deviation. This ranking is fixed before test-set patching and does not use the downstream patching endpoint to rank channels. Alternative AUROC and validation-patching rankings, random controls, and replacement-energy-matched controls are retained as secondary analyses.

Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:probability-error}
E_{\mathrm{prob}}(a,b)=
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lVert p_a^{(i)}-p_b^{(i)}\rVert_2^2.
\end{equation}
denote mean squared probability-space error over the $N$ matched counterfactual pairs. The historical output metric is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:probability-fidelity}
F_{\mathrm{prob}}(S)=
1-\frac{E_{\mathrm{prob}}(S,1)}{E_{\mathrm{prob}}(0,1)}.
\end{equation}
It is a normalized counterfactual reconstruction-error reduction for this patching procedure. It is not interpreted as a mediated fraction or a general measure of causal importance.

Because the denominator is model specific, we later froze two alternative metrics before evaluating them on saved prospective checkpoints. First, centered-logit fidelity subtracts the per-example mean logit,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:centered-logits}
\widetilde{\ell}=\ell-\mathrm{mean}(\ell)\mathbf 1.
\end{equation}
and applies the same normalized error-reduction form in centered-logit space. Second, the unnormalized probability error reduction is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:probability-error-reduction}
R_{\mathrm{prob}}(S)=
E_{\mathrm{prob}}(0,1)-E_{\mathrm{prob}}(S,1).
\end{equation}
Thus $R_{\mathrm{prob}}$ is measured directly in mean squared probability error per matched pair and removes the model-specific denominator entirely.

\subsection{Channel-count contrast and inferential unit}\label{sec:estimand}

For any patching metric $M$, define the within-block treatment difference at channel count $k$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:treatment-difference}
\Delta_b^M(k)=M_b^{\mathrm{annealed}}(k)-M_b^{\mathrm{constant}}(k).
\end{equation}
and the prespecified small-versus-larger-channel contrast
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:channel-count-contrast}
B_b^M=
\frac{\Delta_b^M(4)+\Delta_b^M(8)}{2}
-\frac{\Delta_b^M(1)+\Delta_b^M(2)}{2}.
\end{equation}
By \cref{eq:channel-count-contrast}, a positive $B$ means only that the annealed-minus-constant difference is larger on average at $k=4,8$ than at $k=1,2$; it does not imply monotonicity in $k$ or a positive treatment difference at either larger channel count.

Matched counterfactual image pairs are repeated measurements inside a renderer/model block and are not treated as inferential replicates. Inference is across independently generated renderer blocks. Training conditions are paired within block through shared data and paired initialization components.

\subsection{Statistical design and evidence hierarchy}\label{sec:design}

The final analysis separates developmental evidence from prospectively frozen tests. Early developmental analyses identified that a selected-minus-control comparison could change through the control term and motivated the direct selected-channel contrast in \cref{eq:channel-count-contrast}; those analyses are not pooled with later fresh-sample tests. The primary prospective channel-count experiment uses 20 new \texttt{two\_concepts} renderer blocks. A separately specified schedule/task sensitivity experiment uses 50 fresh blocks per task/architecture setting. The metric robustness analysis reuses saved prospective and sensitivity checkpoints, with the alternative metric definitions frozen before recomputation. The architecture robustness experiment uses 100 further fresh renderer blocks and was prospectively frozen before any of its outcomes were inspected. Finally, an independent checkpoint audit recomputes central quantities at 32 fixed developmental checkpoints. Throughout, inference is at the renderer-block level appropriate to each analysis rather than at the image-pair, initialization, or model-count level.

The architecture robustness experiment preserves the same first-layer 16-channel intervention tensor, template bank, and validation ranking while varying downstream architecture. The ranking remains the same first-layer global-max activation summary for every architecture, including GAP variants, so architecture and channel-selection rule are not changed together. The 16 variants cross global max pooling (GMP) or global average pooling (GAP) with controlled changes in plain depth (1, 2, or 4), downstream width (16 or 64), residual connectivity, and downstream BatchNorm. Only the downstream computation changes; BatchNorm, when present, is after the intervention point.

The architecture robustness experiment trains 100 fresh renderer blocks (6000--6099), four paired initialization replicates per block, two tasks, two treatments, and 16 architectures, for 25,600 trained models. Every first-layer patch budget $k=1,\ldots,16$ is evaluated; the frozen primary contrast $B$ retains the prespecified $k=1,2,4,8$ summary. The four initialization replicates are averaged within renderer block before inference, so the inferential sample size is 100 blocks, not 400 initializations or 25,600 models.

The primary task is \texttt{two\_concepts}. The two primary architecture-robustness metrics are $F_{\mathrm{clogit}}$ and $R_{\mathrm{prob}}$. For each metric, the frozen primary analysis is a one-factor repeated-measures architecture test across the 16 block-level architecture means, followed by a fresh bridge contrast comparing the historical TinyCNN form (\texttt{tiny\_gmp}) with the two-layer form (\texttt{plain2\_w16\_gmp}). The two primary metric tests are Holm adjusted together. Prespecified paired factor contrasts then examine pooling, depth, width, residual connectivity, and BatchNorm.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Template retention is structural, not a proxy for the patching result}\label{sec:structural}

Constant regularization keeps the first-layer filters substantially closer to the predefined bank. In the larger schedule/task sensitivity sample for \texttt{two\_concepts}/TinyCNN, constant $\lambda=1$ regularization gives mean filter-template similarity 0.944, compared with 0.574 under the default annealing schedule, while mean test accuracies are 98.05\% and 99.45\%, respectively. In TwoLayerCNN the corresponding similarities are 0.999 and 0.896, with accuracies 99.11\% and 99.26\%. Thus, greater template retention is not simply a proxy for higher predictive accuracy or for the later patching contrast.

Saved-checkpoint one-to-one matching gives the same qualitative conclusion: the high similarity under constant regularization is not produced merely by repeatedly matching many learned filters to the same few template entries. The complete matching table is retained in \Cref{app:kernel-matching}.

\Cref{fig:kernel-gallery} makes the structural difference visible for one representative \texttt{two\_concepts}/TinyCNN checkpoint. The quantitative claims use the complete replicate-level similarity analyses rather than this example.

\subsection{The patching comparison changes with the number of patched channels}\label{sec:channel-count}

The prospective channel-count protocol froze a single primary test before generating blocks 4000--4019. On \texttt{two\_concepts}/TinyCNN with activation-difference ranking,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:prospective-channel-result}
\bar B_{\mathrm{prob}}=0.330976,
\qquad
95\%\ \mathrm{CI}=[0.273680,0.388273].
\end{equation}
The corresponding test gives $t_{19}=12.09$ and two-sided $p=2.28\times10^{-10}$. All 20 block-level values are positive.

The prospective estimate in \cref{eq:prospective-channel-result} is not a statement that annealed regularization is uniformly better. Under the historical probability metric, the pointwise annealed-minus-constant differences are $-0.3459$, $-0.3317$, $-0.0221$, and $+0.0065$ at $k=1,2,4,8$, respectively. \Cref{fig:main-results}a shows these estimates with their paired 95\% intervals and makes the key pattern visible: the relative treatment effect changes substantially with the number of patched channels. A single patch budget would therefore give an incomplete description of the comparison.

The historical selected-minus-random comparison is not used to define this primary result. In the earlier decomposition, a positive selected-minus-random difference at $k=4$ was partly caused by a lower random-channel baseline under annealing even though the selected-channel patching effect itself was slightly lower. This is why the prospective analysis uses the selected-channel effect directly. Random and replacement-energy-matched controls remain useful diagnostics but answer a different question.

\subsection{The TinyCNN contrast survives alternative output metrics}\label{sec:metric-robustness}

The original $F_{\mathrm{prob}}$ divides by each model's own base-to-counterfactual output difference. A frozen reanalysis of the saved prospective channel-count checkpoints shows that this denominator is not innocuous: release-minus-retention differences in the input-effect scale are substantial. On the prospective TinyCNN setting, the probability-space input-effect scale changes by $+0.8486$ mean squared-error units per pair (95\% CI $[0.8267,0.8704]$), and the centered-logit scale changes by $+126.54$ (95\% CI $[120.85,132.23]$).

The principal channel-count contrast nevertheless remains positive under two alternative output definitions. Centered-logit fidelity gives $B=+0.17888$ (95\% CI $[+0.12727,+0.23049]$; Holm $p=6.95\times10^{-7}$), while the unnormalized probability error reduction gives $B=+0.89441$ (95\% CI $[+0.78630,+1.00252]$; Holm $p=8.59\times10^{-13}$). Centered-logit fidelity retains the normalization form but removes the irrelevant common-logit offset; $R_{\mathrm{prob}}$ removes the model-specific denominator entirely. The three pointwise curves are shown together in \Cref{fig:main-results}a; complete numerical tables are reported in \Cref{app:metric-sensitivity}.

The correct interpretation is therefore not that the original normalization is irrelevant. It changes substantially across treatments. Rather, the TinyCNN small-versus-larger-channel contrast is not explained away by that normalization choice.

\subsection{Architecture strongly conditions the channel-count contrast}\label{sec:architecture}

The architecture robustness experiment tests the architecture question prospectively on 100 fresh renderer blocks. Architecture heterogeneity is large under both frozen primary metrics:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:architecture-omnibus-clogit}
F_{15,1485}=77.09,\quad \eta_p^2=0.438.
\end{equation}
for centered-logit fidelity, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:architecture-omnibus-prob}
F_{15,1485}=656.70,\quad \eta_p^2=0.869.
\end{equation}
for probability error reduction. The Holm-adjusted centered-logit $p$ value is $2.40\times10^{-173}$; the probability-error $p$ value underflows to zero at machine precision.

The fresh bridge contrast in \cref{eq:architecture-bridge} also reproduces the historical TinyCNN-versus-TwoLayer-form difference. For
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:architecture-bridge}
B_{\texttt{tiny\_gmp}}-B_{\texttt{plain2\_w16\_gmp}}.
\end{equation}
The mean difference is $+0.16226$ (95\% CI $[+0.14970,+0.17481]$) for centered-logit fidelity and $+0.88960$ (95\% CI $[+0.86212,+0.91707]$) for probability error reduction.

\Cref{fig:main-results}b shows all 16 primary-task architecture means and intervals. The heterogeneity is visually substantial under both primary metrics, but the pattern is not a single monotone depth or pooling effect. TinyCNN has a much larger positive probability-error $B$ under GMP than GAP, whereas most multi-layer plain and residual backbones show the opposite GAP-minus-GMP direction. The complete numerical architecture table is retained in \Cref{tab:app-architecture-estimates}.

The prespecified factor contrasts sharpen this result. Under GMP, moving from TinyCNN to the two-layer plain form changes $B$ by $-0.16226$ for centered logits and $-0.88960$ for probability error reduction. Adding further plain depth from two to four layers under GMP is approximately null for both metrics. Thus, in this controlled family, the first downstream convolution accounts for nearly all of the Tiny-to-plain depth shift under GMP. Under GAP, depth behaves differently: the two-layer-minus-Tiny contrast is near zero for centered-logit fidelity but positive for probability error reduction, while four-minus-two layers is negative under both metrics.

Pooling itself is a strong modifier but has no architecture-independent direction. All eight prespecified GAP-minus-GMP contrasts are Holm-significant for both primary metrics. For probability error reduction, GAP-minus-GMP ranges from $-0.7372$ in TinyCNN and $-0.0517$ in the depth-2 BatchNorm model to $+0.3929$ in plain depth-2 width-16 and $+0.3268$ in plain depth-2 width-64. Width produces smaller, metric-dependent changes. Residual-versus-plain depth-4 contrasts include zero under both pooling choices and both primary metrics after the frozen family correction. BatchNorm can produce large changes, especially under GAP, but the direction depends on depth and metric.

These results do not identify one architectural mechanism. They establish a narrower point: the relative patching signature of the same first-layer training intervention is strongly conditioned by the downstream computation.

\subsection{Secondary robustness and implementation checks}\label{sec:secondary}

The larger schedule/task sensitivity map gives the same broad warning before the prospective architecture experiment: under the historical contrast ranking, the default annealing schedule produces positive $B$ for TinyCNN on both tasks, while TwoLayerCNN is near zero or negative depending on task and metric. Five alternative regularization schedules preserve the positive TinyCNN probability-fidelity contrast, but these schedules vary both timing and duration and are not interpreted as a dose-response law.

The independent checkpoint audit recomputes central developmental-run metrics at 32 fixed checkpoints without reading the stored scalar outputs first. All values fall within prespecified tolerances; the maximum discrepancy for selected-channel fidelity is $2.39\times10^{-7}$, and full-patch/no-op identity errors are zero. The architecture robustness experiment likewise has maximum full-patch and no-op logit identity errors of zero.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The experiments separate two ideas that are easy to conflate. Constant regularization clearly preserves template-like first-layer weights. Yet the functional comparison between constant and annealed regularization is not a fixed property of those weights: it changes with the number of patched channels and with the downstream architecture through which the same first-layer intervention propagates.

The metric analysis matters for interpretation. The original probability fidelity is normalized by each model's own base-to-counterfactual output change, and that scale differs strongly between treatments. Reporting only the normalized metric would therefore leave open whether the treatment comparison is primarily a reconstruction effect or a normalization effect. The positive TinyCNN $B$ under centered logits and under unnormalized probability error reduction narrows that concern. At the same time, the large architecture-specific differences in output scale mean that absolute $R_{\mathrm{prob}}$ magnitudes should not be compared as though they were architecture-invariant units. Agreement of the two primary metrics on the architecture omnibus and the fresh Tiny-versus-plain2 bridge is more informative than agreement on every secondary factor contrast.

The architecture robustness experiment also argues against a simple depth story. Under GMP, nearly the entire Tiny-to-plain shift appears after adding the first downstream convolution, with little additional change from two to four plain layers. Under GAP, the pattern differs. Pooling has large effects, but their direction reverses across backbones. Residual connectivity is comparatively unimportant in the tested matched comparison, while BatchNorm is strongly context dependent. These interactions are exactly why the result should be described as architecture dependence rather than as a universal effect of one component.

The controlled renderer is a strength for this measurement question because the counterfactual intervention is exact and nuisance variables are matched. It is also a scope restriction. The result is evidence about the behavior of activation-patching comparisons in a controlled small-CNN family, not a claim that natural-image CNNs, larger vision models, or transformers will show the same architecture map.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item The experiments use two related synthetic tasks, one first-layer template bank, one optimizer family, and a controlled small-CNN architecture family. The architecture robustness experiment broadens the architecture test substantially but does not establish natural-image or large-model generalization.
\item Activation patching replaces complete first-layer feature maps and may create activation combinations that are out of distribution for the trained model. The measurements characterize this intervention procedure; they do not identify a unique internal mechanism.
\item The original probability metric has a model-specific normalization denominator that differs across training conditions. We address this directly with centered-logit and unnormalized probability metrics, but no single metric is treated as a privileged causal quantity.
\item The prospective channel-count experiment was frozen in the repository before its fresh blocks were generated but was not externally preregistered. The metric audit was designed after the original probability-space results were known, although the alternative metrics were frozen before their outputs were computed. The architecture robustness experiment was prospectively frozen before any architecture-study outcome was inspected.
\item The architecture robustness experiment contains four initialization replicates per renderer block, but these are averaged before inference. The inferential sample size is 100 fresh renderer blocks per primary architecture comparison, not 400 seeds or 25,600 trained models.
\item The architecture factor contrasts are controlled comparisons within the specified family. Because architecture components interact, they should not be extrapolated as universal causal laws about depth, pooling, normalization, width, or residual connections.
\item Validation data are reused for channel ranking and several secondary diagnostics. Test-set patching remains held out from ranking, but the secondary diagnostics are not independent pieces of evidence.
\item Filter-template similarity is a weight-space score based on centered signed cosine. Even with one-to-one matching, it does not show that filters have unique semantic meanings or that humans find them interpretable.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A structured first-layer prior can strongly preserve predefined filter structure without producing a stable, architecture-independent activation-patching signature. In a prespecified fresh-block TinyCNN test, the release-versus-retention comparison changes sharply between small and larger first-layer channel sets. That channel-count contrast remains under centered-logit fidelity and an unnormalized probability reconstruction-error reduction, despite treatment-dependent normalization scale in the original metric. A separate prospectively frozen study across 16 CNN variants shows that architecture strongly conditions the contrast and prospectively reproduces the earlier TinyCNN-versus-TwoLayer-form difference. Pooling and the first downstream convolution are major modifiers, but their effects interact with the broader architecture and metric. These results support treating intervention size, output metric, and downstream architecture as explicit dimensions of an activation-patching analysis rather than hidden implementation choices, and treating template similarity as a structural measurement distinct from functional intervention behavior.

\section{Data and code availability}\label{sec:availability}

The experiments use procedurally rendered synthetic images. The research artifact contains the renderer, training and evaluation code, frozen protocols, run manifests, complete paper-facing result tables, analysis scripts, and independent checkpoint-audit materials.
