\section{Pre-specified channel-count and schedule/task sensitivity analyses}\label{app:confirmation}

This appendix reports the full numerical results for the pre-specified channel-count experiment and the subsequent schedule/task sensitivity experiment. The channel-count protocol was specified after the earlier analyses but before running blocks 4000--4019, which are disjoint from the earlier data. It was not externally preregistered. The schedule/task sensitivity protocol was separately specified before running blocks 5000--5049 and remains secondary to the channel-count test. Blocks are independent experimental replicates; conditions within a block are paired through shared generated data and relevant initialization components. The main text reports the primary channel-count result and uses the schedule/task analysis as a secondary sensitivity analysis. This appendix preserves the complete channel-selection and schedule breakdowns, secondary controls, and reimplementation details. The complete tables are retained under \texttt{analysis/budget\_confirmation\_001/} and \texttt{analysis/exhaustive\_robustness\_001/}.

\subsection{Pre-specified channel-count contrast}\label{app:prospective-contrast}

For every architecture/channel-selection pair,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-channel-count-contrast}
B=\frac{\Delta F(4)+\Delta F(8)}{2}-\frac{\Delta F(1)+\Delta F(2)}{2}.
\end{equation}
Positive $B$ indicates that the annealed-minus-constant difference is larger on average at $k=4,8$ than at $k=1,2$; it does not imply monotonicity in $k$ and does not require a positive annealing effect at either larger value. The first TinyCNN row, with channels ranked by activation difference, is the single pre-specified primary test. Its 20 block-level $B$ values are all positive (range 0.1452--0.5986). \subsection{Patched-run accuracy on counterfactual targets}\label{app:prospective-cf}

Patched-run accuracy on the counterfactual target was retained alongside the normalized patching effect. For the primary TinyCNN setting, with channels ranked by activation difference, annealed minus constant regularization is $-0.2009$ at $k=1$, $-0.3626$ at $k=2$, $-0.0241$ at $k=4$, and $+0.0140$ at $k=8$. The corresponding 95\% intervals are $[-0.2551,-0.1466]$, $[-0.4400,-0.2852]$, $[-0.0524,0.0042]$, and $[0.0086,0.0193]$.

AUROC and validation-patching channel-selection rules show the same broad change between smaller and larger channel sets. These are secondary outcomes and were not used to define the primary success criterion.

\subsection{Energy-matched control}\label{app:energy-control}

The energy-matched control uses validation replacement energy to select eight alternative channel sets of the same size whose replacement energy is closest to that of the selected set, using validation data only. For TinyCNN with channels ranked by activation difference, the annealed-minus-constant difference in $U_{\mathrm{energy}}$ is -0.2980 at $k=1$, -0.1887 at $k=2$, +0.1310 at $k=4$, and +0.0696 at $k=8$. Matching quality is not uniform: single-channel alternatives can be poorly matched, whereas $k=4$ and $k=8$ are generally much tighter. This analysis shows that the difference relative to the energy-matched baseline also changes with the number of patched channels, but the primary analysis remains the patching effect for selected channels.
